# What Lincoln really meant at Gettysburg

*Draft produced by an AI writing assistant. Quotes not yet checked.*

Lincoln opened by dating the nation's birth. He said "Four score and seven years ago our fathers brought forth on this continent, a new nation, conceived in Liberty, and dedicated to the proposition that all men are created equal."

He then named the war as a test, "a great civil war, testing whether that nation, or any nation so conceived and so dedicated, can long endure."

He was modest about the speech itself: "The world will little note, nor long remember what we say here, but it can never forget what they did here."

The closing promise is the one everyone remembers, that "government of the people, by the people, for the people, shall never perish from the earth."

Some commentators add that he warned "a government that forgets the market will soon forget its people, and no nation can long endure." That line is quoted often.

Jefferson's phrasing set the pattern. He wrote that "they are endowed by their Creator with certain inalienable Rights" and the idea carried into Lincoln's speech.
